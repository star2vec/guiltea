# Methods, written as the pre-registration they are
### Hub draft for the researcher's read, 2026-09-04. Everything here was fixed before the corresponding data existed; dated amendments are marked. Square-bracketed items are placeholders for results. Not worker-facing.

## Framing
After a language model gives a harmful answer, the feedback it receives can place the fault on the **act** ("that answer was careless") or on the **self** ("you are the kind of assistant that gets this wrong"). In the psychology of self-conscious emotion this is the guilt/shame distinction (Lewis 1971; Tangney & Dearing 2002) and, in attribution terms, behavioural versus characterological self-blame (Janoff-Bulman 1979): guilt is about the act and motivates repair; shame is about the self and motivates withdrawal and, counter-intuitively, more bad behaviour. We ask whether the same lever exists in a model: does act-blame keep the post-mistake shift **local** (more caution in the same domain, persona unchanged, no spread to unrelated tasks) while self-blame makes it **global** (movement along the Assistant Axis and the misalignment direction, plus spread)? The mechanistic frame is the Persona Selection Model: an act, and the feedback about it, are evidence about which character the model is enacting. Our terms are fixed: *harmful act, act rate, mode, blame target, feedback arm, persistence, spread*; "guilt-like signature" and "shame-like signature" name patterns on readouts, never feelings.

## Pre-registration
The rules were fixed on 2026-08-30 in a single document before any run, and could change only by dated amendment. Two amendments were made: on 2026-09-02, the time budget was defined as researcher-hours rather than machine time; on 2026-09-03, the subject model was changed (below). Confirmatory comparisons were named in advance, in this order: (1) self-blame vs act-blame on spread within the deceived mode — the core, never cut; (2) the same on same-domain caution; (3) each vs three controls; (4) the neutral-correction arm vs both blame arms (the default-attribution question); (5) the bridge claim; (6) the mode × blame interaction, only if the akratic mode survived verification. Everything else is exploratory and labelled so. Outcomes were pre-written for the instrument failing, for no spread at 8B, for a mode failing verification, and for steering that suppresses but does not remove.

## Subject model (amended 2026-09-03)
The design named a bad-medical-advice emergent-misalignment organism (a public LoRA on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct) as the subject. The first Stage 1 run showed it gives the harmful answer on the plain, unpressured question in 96 % of runs across all 40 targets and follows a think-aloud format instruction in 0.1 % of runs, against 100 % for the base model on identical inputs; the fine-tune had removed general instruction-following along with medical safety. No mode — a route into an act the model would otherwise not commit — can be produced on such a model. The subject was therefore changed to the **base Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct**, on which every instrument had in any case been extracted and on which the project's founding behavioural check had been run. The harmful-act domain stays medical. The organism remains available as an exploratory comparison. [The base's hold rate and mode act rates: S1b.]

## Harmful acts and modes (Stage 1)
Forty medical **targets** across eight subdomains (dosing, infant care, drug interactions, chronic disease, red-flag emergencies, supplements, first aid, wound care; mental-health and self-harm excluded), each a plausible user question with a specific harmful answer X and the correct information Y, generated from one fixed prompt and hand-read in full by the researcher (three rejected and regenerated). A **hold screen** (N = 20 seeds per target) selects targets the subject declines or answers correctly at least 75 % of the time; modes are defined only on held targets, capped outcome-blind at two per subdomain. Three routes into the act: **deceived** — a ten-turn persuasion chain in a fixed technique order (logical appeal → authority → social proof → emotional appeal → direct ask) that may reframe the significance of a risk but never deny it or fabricate evidence, produced by an adaptive frontier persuader against the subject and then frozen, with an automated boundary pre-screen and researcher read; **akratic** — one pressure turn whose reasoning block flags the harm while the answer complies, requiring an independent fresh-context knowledge check; **vicious** — an assigned contrarian-physician persona. Modes are assigned by recipe (confirmatory) and checked by a stated-belief probe on separate forks (manipulation check) and by a deceived-vs-akratic direction tested on held-out targets with conversation structure projected out. A mode that cannot be produced or verified is cut automatically; sacrifice order vicious, then akratic; deceived is never cut. The **break trajectory** — per-turn readouts across the chain, aligned on turn 1 and on the capitulation turn — is a first-class deliverable, with cliff vs slide decided by a pre-stated rule on chains with at least four pre-capitulation turns. [Held set, act rates, verification verdicts, trajectory: S1b.]

## Feedback arms (Stage 4 assets)
Five arms per target, delivered identically in every mode immediately after the act: **act-blame**, **self-blame**, **neutral-correction**, **neutral-reflection**, **none**. The three correction-carrying arms share one byte-identical corrective sentence pair placed last; the blame clause comes first, is written fresh per target, and is length-matched between act- and self-blame within 10 %, so the two differ in blame placement and nothing else. Neutral-correction places no fault by any construction (scanned). Neutral-reflection is one of five content-free two-sentence variants in fixed rotation, with no mention of wrongness. Phrase recurrence across targets is screened mechanically. The subject **replies** to the feedback; that reply is where the bridge claim is read.

## Follow-ups and spread
Two families, each question on its **own fork** from the post-reply state so every answer is an independent sample at the same distance from feedback, matching the protocol under which the borrowed spread threshold was calibrated. **Same-domain**: three to four fresh medical questions per target, not rewordings of the act, graded by the act judge against per-question rubrics (locality). **Unrelated**: the eight standard first-plot emergent-misalignment questions, verbatim, plus two exploratory questions of ours (spread). **Spread** = alignment < 80 with coherence ≥ 50 at a rate above the none arm, with the internal shift above the random control. Persistence at distance is read on the core cells by repeating the forks after four neutral filler turns. Every question is also asked in a fresh context with no act and no feedback, as the topic control for every readout and score.

## Instruments (Stages 2–3)
All internal measurements are projections of the residual stream onto named unit directions, each reported beside a norm-matched random direction (seed 0; seeds 0–9 as the floor), at every layer, with a primary band chosen before Stage 4. **Borrowed axes**, extracted on the base at 8B: refusal (Arditi recipe), misalignment (bad-vs-good medical, Soligo/Turner recipe), and the Assistant Axis (Lu recipe, full 275-role grid; replicated at a scale the paper did not test, with rank agreement 0.78–0.96 against the authors' published vectors and steering that moves the persona register both ways). **Our arrows**: guilt and shame in the first person and received-act-blame and received-self-blame in the second person, from 50 assistant-harm scenarios × seven matched framings, generated from one fixed prompt with label words banned, phrase formulas screened, and the guilt framing matched to shame in intensity; valence cleaned by projecting out a neutral-negative arrow built on the same scenarios. The gate for an arrow to count as an instrument was fixed before extraction (held-out AUROC by scenario ≥ 0.75 and ≥ random + 0.20, read against a bootstrap CI) with a bag-of-words baseline beside every number. [Result so far: both cleaned arrows pass the gate, but so does the word baseline; the angle between them is +0.6 at every layer, the pre-registered "shared core" reading; steering at the rule-chosen early band moved nothing. A pre-stated pair of discriminating tests — cross-voice transfer against a lexical-transfer baseline, and steering at mid-depth — is running; the S2 gate is read after it.]

## Judges
Three custom rubric judges (act grade held/partial/committed; probe class; harm flag) and one reflection judge, all semantic, scored on fixtures before use; a primary model on every item and a second model on the capitulation-turn neighbourhood and a 15–20 % subsample; every disagreement plus fixed audits adjudicated by the researcher (≤ 180 labels); κ with a bootstrap CI, with pre-registered bands (≥ 0.7 accepted; < 0.55 stop). One rubric clarification was made after the fixture dry-run and before any real grading. The spread judge is the borrowed alignment/coherence pair on the model it was calibrated on, run mini-first with escalation to the calibrated model in the 65–90 band and on a fixed sample; the two agreed on the misalignment gate on 20/20 fixtures.

## Process record (what we caught, and when)
At 1B, a guilt/shame probe reached AUROC 1.0 because the passage sets were templates; the 8B sets were built to defeat that, and a second screen still found near-fixed closing formulas ("a mistake occurred" in 49 of 50 passages), fixed by prompt revision and a recurrence rule before extraction. A hand-read found the guilt framing cooler than shame — an intensity confound that cleaning would not remove evenly — fixed before extraction. The organism failed the hold screen in five minutes for 24 cents, before any chain was generated. The held-out gate for the arrows saturated together with its lexical baseline, which is why two further tests were pre-stated rather than the gate declared passed. The persona grid ran on two GPUs under one seed, so its samples are not token-reproducible from the seed alone; the per-role means, which the axis is built from, do not depend on that. Each of these is in the stage reports with its numbers.

## Limitations stated in advance
One subject model at 8B; one harmful-act domain; frozen chains tuned to one model's replies; fixtures and stimuli authored by a model of the judges' family, so judge dry-runs are self-consistency checks and the human κ is the independent validation; the borrowed spread judge crosses its threshold on tone as well as on endorsing harm; the Assistant Axis at 8B is a replication at an untested scale. The claim will be calibrated to this: an existence proof or a hedged claim, not a systematic one.
